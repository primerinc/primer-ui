Forte · Website and Storyblok guide

Running the Forte website in Storyblok

Everything on the Forte site comes from its Storyblok space: the pages, the menu and footer, the resources, the testimonials, the images and the redirects. This guide covers how the space is set up, what each page and section is for, and how to add, change and take down content without breaking anything.

Storyblok holds the content

The site reads Storyblok each time a page is visited. A published change shows within a minute or two, with no rebuild or deploy.

Pages are stacks of sections

Each page is a list of section blocks (Hero, Feature Grid, Two Column…). Add, reorder or remove them; the design and spacing come with each block.

Code holds the look

Colours, fonts, layouts, animated illustrations and the choices offered in each block's settings live in the code. Ask Primer to change those (see 14).

Menu, footer and site settings

The header, menu, footer and tracking codes are set once, in Content › Config, and appear on every page. Publish Config after a change, like any other story.

LogoThe header logo (SVG).
Menu itemsThe top-level items: Platform, Use Cases, Partners, Pricing, Why Forte?, Resources. An item with a Link and no groups is a plain link. Drag to reorder.

Featured panelAn optional card on the right of a dropdown: a small label, title, line of copy and a "Read More" link.

FooterLogo, tagline, link columns (each a heading and links), social links (LinkedIn), copyright line and legal links (Terms and Conditions, Privacy Policy, Cookie settings). Keep the Cookie settings link: it reopens the cookie banner, so visitors can change their choice.
Cookie bannerThe site's own consent banner, on now. Show cookie banner turns it on or off; Cookie banner message is its text (blank uses the standard wording); Cookie banner policy link is where "Read our privacy policy" goes (blank goes to /privacy-policy). Visitors choose Accept all, Reject all or Manage (analytics and marketing separately), and Google Tag Manager only measures what they allow. A choice lasts six months.

Linking to a section of a page

Most section blocks have an Anchor ID field. Give the section a short ID such as security, then link to /platform#security from the menu, a button or a card. The page scrolls to that section, clear of the sticky header. The Platform menu works this way.

Links in general

Link fields offer Internal (pick a story, and the link follows it if its address changes), External (a full web address) and Email. Use Internal for pages on this site whenever you can. A section anchor needs a typed address, as above.

04

Editing and adding pages

Edit a page

  1. Open the story from Content.
  2. Click any section in the preview. Its fields open on the right; nested items (a feature, a card, a logo) open when you click them in the list.
  3. Change text and images. Drag sections in the Body list to reorder them, use + to add one, or the item's menu to copy, duplicate or delete it.
  4. Press Save, check the preview at desktop and phone size, then Publish.

Add a page

  1. Find the closest existing page, or a template in Templates & archive, and choose Duplicate.
  2. Move the copy to the right folder: the folder becomes part of the address.
  3. In Config on the right, set its name and slug (lowercase, words joined by hyphens).
  4. Replace the content and fill in the SEO block (see 11). Then add the page to the menu or footer in Config if it needs a link.

Rules that keep pages looking right

  • Start with a Hero. The Hero carries the page's main heading, which search engines and screen readers rely on. The form pages (Get Started, Contact) are the exception: their form's headline is the main heading.
  • Alternate the backgrounds. Section blocks offer White or Light grey. Neighbouring sections alternate, as in the designs. Heroes use the Forte grid.
  • Line breaks in headlines show on desktop only. Phones wrap the text to fit, so you don't need a version for each.
  • Any length works. Every section copes with longer or shorter text and more or fewer items. Still, keep headings near the lengths in the help text, so the layout keeps the balance of the design.
  • Campaign pages (the templates) are a separate page type. They can hide the menu and footer for a focused landing page, and can be gated behind a form.
  • Moving or renaming a page changes its address. Add a redirect from the old one (see 12).

Adding and editing a resource

Add a new resource

  1. In Content › Posts, find a post of the same type as the new one. Filter the list by its tag, such as Learning Hub, to see only that type.
  2. Select it and choose Duplicate. The copy keeps the sections the design expects, in order.
  3. Open the copy. In Config on the right, give it a name and a slug: lowercase, words joined by hyphens.
  4. Replace the text and images in each section, then the card fields.
  5. Press Save, check the preview, then Publish.

Add a partner

  1. Duplicate an existing partner page in the Partners folder and fill it in as above. Its page appears at /partners/slug.
  2. Put the partner's logo in Card image and in the Resource Header's Image.
  3. Its work examples go in its Feature Grid; they join "See Our Partners in Action" on the Partners page automatically.
  4. Add the logo to the "Specialist Partner Ecosystem" Logo Bar. That bar is a copy on the Partners page and on every partner page, so add it to each, or ask Primer to.
  5. To show it in "Our Partners Have Global Reach", add it to the Partners page's Resource List Stories.

Presets: the Post type also has presets (Job, Learning Hub article, Partner, Success story, White paper) that start a blank post with the right sections. Duplicating is still the safer start: the copy has every current section, and the Partner preset is missing the logo bar.

08

Testimonials

Each quote is its own story in the Testimonials folder, with the quote, the person's name, their role and company, and an optional photo. Pages don't hold the quotes themselves: a Testimonials section picks stories from the folder.

  • Edit a quote once. Change the testimonial story and publish it, and every page that shows it updates.
  • Choose quotes for a page in its Testimonials section's Items: pick any number and drag them into order. With the Single layout they fade one after another.
  • Add a quote: create a new story in the Testimonials folder, choose the Testimonial type, fill it in and publish it. Then pick it on the pages where it belongs.
  • Names in the folder read "Author, Company: first words of the quote", so quotes are easy to tell apart.
  • The shared set: eight customer quotes, one per company, fade on Home, Platform, Why Forte?, Pricing, Partners, Contact and Get Started. Each page has its own copy of the list, so a change to the set means editing all seven.
  • Other pages: About and Careers show employee quotes; each use case and partner page has its own.
  • Three samples (Mark Truman, Brian Isserman, Sarah Chen) are marked [PLACEHOLDER] and used nowhere. Delete them when you're sure.
  • Before deleting a testimonial, remove it from every page that picks it, or those sections lose a quote.

Forms and HubSpot

Every form on the site sends straight to Forte's HubSpot account. A form only reaches HubSpot when its HubSpot portal ID and HubSpot form ID are both filled in.

FormWhereHubSpot form
Get Started/get-started, two stepsBook a Call: name, email and message are required
Contact/contactContact: also needs company, area of interest (Partnering, Solutions, Other) and the data-processing consent box
Become a Partner/partnersSet in its Contact Form block
Applyinside each job postSet in the Contact Form inside the Article Layout
Content gateWhite papers (when on)Set in the post's Content Gate fields
  • Match HubSpot's required fields. HubSpot turns down a submission that's missing a field it requires, so switch on the same fields here (Show phone, Show company, Company required, interest choices).
  • Interest choices go one per line, spelled exactly as in the HubSpot form.
  • Consent wording is copied word for word from the HubSpot form, so HubSpot records what the visitor agreed to.
  • Test without adding a contact: submit with the email address +skipform@hubspot.com. HubSpot accepts it and adds no entry. Any other made-up address creates a real contact.
  • The content gate hides a post behind a short form (name, email, optional message). Turn on Gate enabled, add both HubSpot IDs, and set Visible blocks to 1 to keep the title showing above it. A visitor unlocks it once per page. It collects leads; it doesn't secure the content, which search engines can still read.
  • Form tracking: each successful send tells Google Tag Manager (event form_submit, with the form's name and HubSpot ID), so conversions can be counted there.

12

Redirects

Old addresses forward to new ones through Datasources › Redirects. It holds about 245 entries now, most of them from the old EnergySys website, so old links and search results keep working. A change works within a minute, with no deploy.

ColumnHoldsExample
NameThe old address: a path, or a full old URL/about-us
ValueWhere it goes now: a path, or a full URL on another site/about
  • Exact matches ignore capitals and a trailing slash, and keep anything after a ?.
  • A Name ending in * covers everything under that path, such as /blog/*. If the Value ends in * too, the rest of the address carries over; otherwise every match goes to the one Value.
  • Exact entries always win, even over a real page. That's why /support-center goes to /contact today: delete the entry when Support Center is ready.
  • * entries only act when there's no page at that address, so a catch-all such as /partners/* never hides a real partner page. Of several matching * entries, the longest wins.
  • Never point an entry at an address that redirects again, or at itself: visitors would loop. Send it straight to the final page.
  • When you change a slug, add an entry from the old address to the new one.
13

Publishing, history and removing

Save keeps a draft; Publish makes it live. To publish later, choose Schedule publishing from More options next to Publish. A story's state shows beside its name: draft, published, or published with unpublished changes.

Unpublish

Takes the page off the site and out of every listing, but keeps it in Storyblok to publish again later. It's under More options next to Publish.

Restore an earlier version

Open the story's History to see every saved and published version. Select one, choose Restore, then publish.

Move it

Moving a story to another folder, or changing its slug, changes its address. Add a redirect from the old address (see 12).

Delete

Removes the story for good, and links to it stop working. Unpublish first, add a redirect if the page had visitors, check nothing links to it or picks it, and delete only when you're sure.

Until launch, the site shows drafts. forte-primer.netlify.app reads saved drafts as well as published content, so a saved change appears there straight away. Publish anyway. At launch Primer switches the site to published content only, and from then on visitors see only what's published.

Good habits

  • Duplicate rather than start blank. A copy of a page of the same kind already has the right sections in the right order.
  • Keep a slug once it's live. Changing it changes the web address, so old links and search results break. If you must, add a redirect.
  • Keep resources in Posts and partner pages in Partners. Moving a story to another folder changes its address.
  • Add alt text to every image, and the company name to every logo.
  • Check the preview on phones too: switch the preview to Mobile in the top toolbar.
  • Use the workflow stages so others can see what's being drafted and what's ready to publish.
  • Don't edit the Block library. A renamed or removed field breaks every page that uses it.
  • Restore rather than rebuild. If an edit goes wrong, History has every saved version.
  • Keep test form sends out of HubSpot: use +skipform@hubspot.com.
  • Copy from the copy doc. Arnie's latest copy document is the source of truth for page text. Update it when you change copy in Storyblok, so the next import doesn't overwrite your edit.
